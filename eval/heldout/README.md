## Protocol

1. **Strata.** Seven slots: machine learning, computer vision and astrophysics from arXiv (read from LaTeX source), and medicine (PLOS ONE), biology (eLife) and psychology (Frontiers in Psychology, PLOS ONE) read from PDF. Six were filled (see below). Every paper is open access under CC BY, so the edited texts in this folder can be shared.
2. **Sampling.** For each stratum, a random sample of 25 papers from OpenAlex with a fixed seed (42; then 43 and 44 if no paper in a sample qualifies, a rule fixed before it was needed), screened in sample order. The first paper that passes is taken. Every decision and its reason is in `manifest.json`.
3. **Eligibility.** 25–90 citing sentences; at least half of the in-text citations matched to a reference entry; at least half of the cited references with open full text (checked first against OpenAlex, then by actually resolving them); not a paper used while building, debugging or calibrating the checker.
4. **Planting.** A full check of the unmodified paper, then up to ten errors planted on citations it judged supported with high confidence and no tags (number changes, scope inflation, hedge removal, citation swaps; `app/planting.py`). Same procedure as the demo paper, with the two planting fixes listed below. The evaluation check of each edited paper reuses the baseline's resolved references, so the two runs differ only by the planted edits.
5. **Scoring.** A full check of the edited paper. A planted error counts as caught if its citation is flagged (contradicted, not found, overstated, or tagged as possibly the wrong reference). Every flag on an untouched citation is reviewed by hand against the cited text and marked genuine, false alarm or debatable (`<paper>/annotations.json`, with notes; review sheets in `<paper>/review.md`).
6. **No tuning.** The pipeline is frozen from the first planting to the last score. The checker's prompts, rules and thresholds were not changed in response to anything seen on these papers.

## Deviations and caveats

- **A mislabelled stratum.** The first eligible paper in the machine-learning sample is about quantum computing (OpenAlex files it under Artificial Intelligence). It was kept, as the protocol requires, and is labelled by its real field.
- **Resolver change during screening.** Screening the medicine stratum showed that the resolver obtained far less open full text than OpenAlex lists, because publisher sites refuse automated downloads. Europe PMC full text was added to the resolver before anything was planted or scored, and the candidates rejected for low coverage were screened again with it. This is a coverage fix, not a judge change; it is recorded in `manifest.json`.
- **Planting only on clean citations.** Errors are planted where the checker already read the source correctly, so recall here measures detection of a change in a citation the checker can read. It does not measure citations whose sources the checker cannot read or misreads.
- **One reviewer.** Flags are reviewed by one reviewer (Claude, reading the cited text), not by domain experts or several annotators. Debatable cases are left out of precision rather than counted either way.
- **Empty stratum.** The Frontiers in Psychology stratum had no eligible paper in 75 candidates (73 had open full text for fewer than half of their references). Psychology is represented by the PLOS ONE paper.
- **Planting fixes before any evaluation run.** The first planting pass gave only 36 errors, because citation swaps could only use each citation's single least related reference and those collided. Swaps may now use any of the 5 least related references (still at least 0.15 less similar than the original, each used once). Reading the edits also showed ML-specific scope phrases ("across all tasks and model sizes" in a malaria paper) and an ungrammatical hedge deletion ("most likely" → "most"); the held-out set uses field-neutral phrases and skips that deletion. All of this happened before any edited paper was checked; the demo paper keeps the original rules.
- **Few numbers.** Only one number change could be planted: in these fields, numbers in citing sentences rarely appear verbatim in the cited text the checker reads. Citation swaps (29 of 44) dominate the planted set.
- **Small sample.** 44 planted errors and 63 reviewed flags across 6 papers: the confidence intervals are wide.

## Findings

**Recall (35 of 44).** The 9 misses:

- **4 are caused by a calibration rule.** On the demo paper, "identification" citations (a citation that names a tool, dataset or instrument) were made lenient, which removed many false alarms. Here it hides 4 citation swaps: the judge said, correctly, that the swapped-in paper never mentions KCWI, PyNeb, the cited G6PD assay or Photovoice, and the rule still softened the verdict to "could not be verified".
- **1 background-citation leniency, 1 abstract-only reference** (a scope inflation the abstract cannot settle).
- **3 judge misses:** a scope inflation ("without exception") and a hedge removal ("may fail" → "will fail") judged supported, and one swap where the swapped-in paper also supports the claim (arguably not an error).

**Precision (54%).** Of 34 false alarms, 5 are caused by the checker's inputs, not its judgement:

- a DOI split across a line break in a PDF reference list ("journal.pmed. 1002151") resolved to an unrelated paper;
- two generic titles matched to the wrong paper ("Orthogonal Polynomials"; "The Lyman alpha reference sample" via the arXiv-preprint lookup);
- the LaTeX converter drops `\lesssim`/`\gtrsim`, so "z ≲ 0.2" reached the judge as "redshift 0.2";
- table contents of cited papers are not in the checker's text, so numbers reported only in tables look absent.

The other 29 are the judge being too literal: expecting a method, data-source or definition citation to contain the citing authors' own results or comparisons (most of the biology false alarms), reading a citation as attached to the wrong part of a sentence, and 3 number-drift tags where the numbers agree ("18 kpc" for 18.7; ratios the judge itself called supported).

**Genuine problems found in the original papers (5):** a stigma definition cited to a study that never defines stigma; a Jordanian mental-health survey cited for an effect of stigma it never mentions; a "first" attribution that the cited paper itself credits to earlier work; a 17.5 kpc scale length credited to a study that measured 15.6 kpc (17.5 is another study's value); and a citation for "no redshift evolution" to a paper that reports a significant decrease with redshift.

**Version mismatch.** The checker reads the arXiv version when one exists. For nnU-Net it read the 2019 preprint while the citing paper cites the 2021 journal version with updated numbers; those flags are marked debatable.

**Fields.** Precision ranges from 31% (biology, dense method and data-source citations) to 91% (psychology). Coverage of open full text, not the judge, decides which papers can be checked at all: most medical and psychology papers fail the 50% bar.
